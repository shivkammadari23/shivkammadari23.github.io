PRODUCT REQUIREMENTS DOCUMENT

Service Operations Exception Triage — MVP

Goal and users

Goal: Identify and triage work-order exceptions faster while keeping consequential decisions with people.

Dispatchers/coordinators: Find exceptions, inspect context, record review or defer decisions. Operations leads: Understand regional capacity pressure and unresolved work. Service managers: Review trends and dispositions.

Core workflow

  1. Read approved work-order, due-window, region, capacity, and dependency signals.
  2. Flag a possible exception with a reason, source, and timestamp.
  3. Inspect evidence before action.
  4. Mark reviewed, defer with rationale, or route for follow-up.
  5. Record reviewer, action, time, rationale, and outcome in production.

Non-goals

No autonomous dispatch/reassignment, no replacement of the system of record, no unsupported prediction claims, and no irreversible updates without confirmation.

Prioritized requirements and acceptance criteria

PriorityRequirementAcceptance criterion
P0Exception queueEach item shows issue, region, priority, reason, and due/capacity context.
P0Inspectable evidenceUser sees triggering signal, source, and timestamp before acting.
P0Human controlNo assignment or update without explicit action and confirmation.
P1Severity filtersQueue and result count update together.
P1Review dispositionProduction version records reviewer, action, timestamp, and defer/override rationale.
P2Regional trendsAuthorized users compare exception volume and resolution time by period.

Success metrics and guardrails

Primary: median time to first meaningful triage action, prioritization accuracy against a reviewed reference set, and share triaged within the required window. Guardrails: missed high-severity exceptions, false positives, reopened/deferred items, override rate, stale data, and actions without sufficient evidence.

Discovery and validation

Interview representative dispatchers and operations leads; map the current workflow and escalation rules; then run task-based prototype sessions with non-sensitive scenarios. Measure triage time, prioritization accuracy, error detection, and whether users can explain why an item was flagged. Set baselines before targets; no impact is claimed for this prototype.

Open questions: source of truth, severity definitions, escalation rules, data latency, permissions, audit requirements, and integrations.

CASE STUDY 02 · APPLIED AI RESEARCH

Explainable AI for human review

Research context

At the University of South Florida, I investigated domain shift and transferability in deep-learning medical-image analysis using the NIH ChestX-ray14 dataset. I adapted a ResNet-based approach into IMPACT-Net and developed normalization, augmentation, and balanced-sampling workflows to study generalization across data sources.

Grad-CAM visualizations were integrated to inspect image regions influencing model predictions and make aspects of model behavior more interpretable during review.

View research code ↗

Product implications

  • Inspectable evidence: show relevant context alongside a prediction.
  • Visible limitations: communicate intended use and domain constraints.
  • Clear decision rights: let qualified reviewers reject or escalate suggestions.
  • Workflow-level evaluation: measure comprehension, review time, error detection, and over-reliance as well as model performance.
Evidence boundary: Grad-CAM is an inspection aid, not proof that a prediction is correct or clinically safe. This work is research, not a clinical deployment. No patient-level images or identifiable records are published here.
