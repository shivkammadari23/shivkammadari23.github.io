\section{Problem and target users}
Operations teams coordinating work orders must balance due windows, regional demand, capacity, and changing information. Static reports may show volume without making exceptions requiring immediate review obvious. The concept is a human-in-the-loop queue for dispatchers/coordinators (find exceptions and understand why flagged), operations leads (see capacity pressure), and service managers (maintain accountability).\\
\textit{Evidence note:} This product translation is informed by paid operational work, including analysis of real customer and client operational records in enterprise workflow settings. The requirements below translate observed operational problems into a proposed decision-support workflow; no customer- or client-level records are reproduced in this document.
\section{Workflow and scope}
\begin{enumerate}\item Read approved work-order status, due window, region, capacity, and dependency signals.\item Flag possible exceptions with a plain-language reason and source timestamp.\item Let users inspect evidence and linked work orders before acting.\item Allow an explicit human decision: mark reviewed, defer with reason, or route for follow-up.\item Log reviewer, action, time, rationale, and outcome when implemented in an authorized operational system.\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Non-goals:} automatic dispatch/reassignment; replacing the system of record; unsupported predictive claims; irreversible updates without confirmation.

\section{Success metrics and guardrails}
\textbf{Primary:} median time to first meaningful triage action; prioritization accuracy against a reviewed reference set; share of exceptions triaged within the required window.\\
\textbf{Guardrails:} missed high-severity exceptions, false-positive rate, reopened/deferred items, override rate, stale data exposure, and actions without sufficient evidence. Set baselines and targets after measuring the current workflow; no impact result is claimed for this proposed product workflow.
\section{Discovery and validation plan}
Prior workflow-testing experience across the related American Express and Powerhouse workstreams included alpha testing with 50 internal participants and nearly 200 users across UAT, task-based usability testing, and observation combined. These are aggregate figures across both workstreams, not per-company counts. The operational analyses and workflow-testing activities were conducted in real work settings and used real customer/client operational records where required for the work. For this proposed workflow, discovery should map current handoffs and escalation rules with dispatchers and operations leads, then evaluate the requirements against approved operational scenarios. Measure time-to-triage, prioritization accuracy, error detection, and whether users can explain why a record was flagged; iterate based on observed errors.
\section{Open questions}
Which system is the source of truth? Who defines severity? What data latency or missingness makes recommendations unsafe? Which exceptions require escalation? Which access controls, audit requirements, and integrations are mandatory?
